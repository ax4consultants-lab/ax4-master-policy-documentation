## 1. Purpose

This SHEQ Management Manual explains how Ax4 Consulting Pty Ltd, trading as Ax4, Ax4 Asbestos Consultants and Ax4 WHS Consultants, manages safety, health, environmental and quality requirements across its consulting operations.

The manual links Ax4's corporate policy commitments to the procedures, templates, forms, registers and technical controls used to plan, perform, review and improve work.

This manual is intended to support:

- South Australian WHS compliance;
- asbestos and hazardous materials consulting work;
- client and tender prequalification requirements;
- safe and consistent field operations;
- technically accurate and legally defensible reporting;
- controlled document production;
- future automation through Ax4-controlled systems and workflows.

This manual does not replace site-specific planning, client requirements, legislative duties, technical procedures or professional judgement. It provides the management system framework that links those controls together.

---

## 2. Scope of the SHEQ System

The Ax4 SHEQ system applies to work performed by or on behalf of Ax4, including:

- asbestos inspections, surveys, register reviews and management plan work;
- asbestos register and risk assessment reporting;
- sampling coordination and sample handling activities;
- pre-refurbishment and pre-demolition advice;
- hazardous materials assessments, including lead, silica, synthetic mineral fibre and mould where within scope;
- WHS consulting and compliance documentation;
- contractor and technical partner coordination;
- site attendance, travel, fieldwork, office work and client communication;
- report preparation, review, issue, retention and revision;
- controlled policy, procedure, form, register and template management.

The system applies to:

- directors and officers;
- workers;
- contractors and consultants engaged by Ax4;
- technical partners, including licensed asbestos assessors, occupational hygienists, laboratories and specialist consultants where engaged;
- any person performing work under Ax4's direction or control.

The system is scaled for a small South Australian consultancy and is intended to be practical, auditable and proportionate to the risk and complexity of the work being performed.

## 3. System Structure

Ax4's SHEQ documentation is structured in layers.

| Layer | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate Policies | State Ax4's commitments and governance intent | WHS Policy, Environmental Policy, Quality Policy, Integrated SHEQ Policy Statement |
| Management Manual | Explains how the SHEQ system operates and links documents | This manual |
| Procedures | Define how controlled activities are performed | Document control, incident reporting, contractor management, asbestos inspection procedures |
| Templates / Forms | Capture planning, site control and evidence | SHEP, JSA, SWMS, induction records, emergency response forms |
| Registers | Track evidence and status | Training register, document register, corrective action register |
| Libraries | Standardise wording and decision logic | Report limitations, recommended action system |

The system follows the principle that:

- policies set commitment;
- the manual explains the system;
- procedures define how work is done;
- SHEP, SWMS and JSA documents control site execution;
- forms and registers prove the work occurred;
- libraries standardise wording and automation logic.

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

### 6.1 Director / Senior Management

The Director is responsible for:

- approving SHEQ policies and controlled documents;
- ensuring the SHEQ system is suitable for Ax4's work;
- ensuring adequate resources are available to manage SHEQ risks;
- ensuring workers and contractors are competent for assigned tasks;
- reviewing significant incidents, hazards, complaints and nonconformances;
- supporting corrective actions and continual improvement;
- ensuring legal and client requirements are considered during planning and delivery.

### 6.2 Project Lead / Consultant

The project lead or consultant is responsible for:

- confirming scope and client requirements before work begins;
- identifying relevant hazards, risks and site constraints;
- preparing or confirming required SHEP, JSA, SWMS or site-control documents;
- ensuring work is performed within competence and agreed scope;
- confirming PPE/RPE and equipment requirements;
- documenting site observations, limitations, photographs, samples and decisions;
- escalating uncontrolled risks, access issues, scope changes or urgent hazards;
- preparing technically accurate and controlled reports;
- ensuring required review or approval occurs before issue.

### 6.3 Workers and Contractors

Workers and contractors are responsible for:

- following Ax4 policies, procedures and site instructions;
- working within their competence;
- using required PPE/RPE and equipment correctly;
- participating in consultation, inductions and pre-start briefings;
- reporting hazards, incidents, near misses, nonconformances and quality concerns;
- stopping work where conditions are unsafe or outside agreed controls;
- protecting client sites and avoiding unnecessary disturbance, contamination or damage.

### 6.4 Technical Partners

Technical partners may include licensed asbestos assessors, occupational hygienists, laboratories, licensed asbestos removalists, specialist consultants or other competent persons.

Technical partners are responsible for:

- maintaining relevant licences, insurances and competence;
- working within agreed scope;
- providing accurate records, results, reports or advice;
- complying with legal, site and Ax4 requirements relevant to their work;
- notifying Ax4 of issues that may affect safety, quality, scope, timing or report accuracy.

### 6.5 Clients and Duty Holders

Clients and workplace duty holders remain responsible for their own legal duties unless otherwise agreed in writing and legally appropriate.

This may include responsibility for:

- providing accurate site information;
- allowing safe and suitable access;
- notifying occupants, workers or contractors where required;
- implementing asbestos management plans and recommended controls;
- maintaining registers and management plans where they are the person with management or control of the workplace;
- engaging licensed removalists or other specialists where required;
- implementing site controls before work proceeds.

Ax4 will provide consulting advice within the agreed scope but does not assume control of client duty-holder obligations by issuing a report or recommendation.

## 7. Risk Management Process

Ax4 will manage SHEQ risk through a structured risk management process.

The process includes:

1. identifying the task, location, scope and affected persons;
2. identifying hazards and potential impacts;
3. assessing risk based on likely consequence and likelihood;
4. selecting controls using the hierarchy of control;
5. documenting controls in the appropriate planning document;
6. communicating controls to workers and contractors;
7. monitoring the work and applying dynamic risk assessment where conditions change;
8. reviewing controls after incidents, changes, feedback or completion.

Risk controls must be proportionate to the task, site and likely exposure.

Where a risk cannot be adequately controlled, work must not proceed until the risk is eliminated, reduced or escalated for further decision.

### 7.1 Hierarchy of Control

Ax4 will consider the hierarchy of control when selecting controls:

1. eliminate the hazard where reasonably practicable;
2. substitute the hazard or work method;
3. isolate people from the hazard;
4. apply engineering controls;
5. apply administrative controls;
6. use PPE/RPE as the final layer of control.

PPE/RPE must not be treated as the only control where higher-order controls are reasonably practicable.

### 7.2 Dynamic Risk Assessment

Workers and contractors must reassess the work where:

- site conditions differ from expected conditions;
- access is restricted, unsafe or incomplete;
- suspected asbestos, dust, debris or other hazardous material is discovered;
- client or contractor activities create new risks;
- weather, lighting, traffic, plant, electrical services or occupancy conditions change;
- the work is outside the agreed scope or competence;
- existing controls are not effective.

Where required, work must stop and the issue must be escalated to the project lead or Director.

## 11. Incident, Hazard and Near Miss Management

Ax4 will maintain a process for reporting and managing incidents, hazards and near misses.

Reportable matters may include:

- injury or illness;
- asbestos disturbance or suspected disturbance;
- unexpected asbestos-contaminated dust or debris;
- uncontrolled exposure risk;
- unsafe access, roof space or confined/restricted area conditions;
- vehicle or traffic incidents;
- environmental spill or contamination;
- damage to client property;
- significant report error or quality nonconformance;
- contractor or site control failure.

The incident process must include:

- immediate response and make-safe action;
- notification to the project lead or Director;
- client or site notification where required;
- consideration of notifiable incident obligations;
- investigation proportionate to severity;
- corrective action and close-out;
- record keeping and lessons learned.

Detailed requirements will be controlled in PRO-INC-001 — Incident, Hazard and Near Miss Reporting Procedure once developed.

Ax4 will identify PPE, RPE and equipment requirements before and during work.

PPE/RPE may include, where relevant:

- safety footwear;
- high-visibility clothing;
- eye protection;
- gloves;
- disposable coveralls;
- hearing protection;
- respiratory protective equipment;
- sun protection;
- first aid kit;

- sealable sample bags;

## 14. Asbestos and Hazardous Materials Controls

Ax4's work may involve identifying, assessing or advising on asbestos-containing materials and other hazardous materials.

Ax4 will apply controlled language and safe work principles when dealing with asbestos and hazardous materials.

### 14.1 Asbestos Consulting Controls

Asbestos-related work must consider:

- agreed scope and purpose;
- accessible and inaccessible areas;
- visual, semi-intrusive or intrusive inspection limits;
- sampling requirements and client approval where applicable;
- suspected, presumed and confirmed asbestos-containing materials;
- condition, accessibility and disturbance risk;
- signage, labelling and management controls;
- client duty-holder responsibilities;
- need for licensed removalist, hygienist or assessor involvement;
- report limitations and reliance rules.

Ax4 must not imply that:

- a site is asbestos-free;
- all asbestos will be identified;
- concealed asbestos can be located by visual inspection;
- sampling is always included;
- Ax4 guarantees compliance outcomes;
- Ax4 controls client duty-holder obligations.

### 14.2 Sampling Controls

Sampling is not automatic.

Sampling depends on:

- agreed scope;
- safety;
- site access;
- practicality;
- risk;
- client approval where applicable;
- whether representative sampling can be undertaken without unacceptable disturbance.

Where samples are collected, they must be appropriately labelled, sealed, recorded and submitted to a suitable laboratory. Where samples are not collected, reports must clearly state the basis for any suspected or presumed findings.

### 14.3 Hazardous Materials Controls

Where lead, silica, synthetic mineral fibre, mould, dust, contaminated waste or other hazards are within scope, Ax4 will identify the applicable limitations, competence requirements, control measures and specialist input required.

Where a hazard is outside Ax4's agreed scope or competence, Ax4 will advise the client and recommend further specialist assessment where appropriate.

## 15. Environmental Management

Ax4 will conduct its work in a manner that prevents or minimises environmental harm so far as is reasonably practicable.

Environmental controls may include:

- avoiding unnecessary disturbance of suspect materials;
- preventing dust, debris or sample residues from spreading;
- using appropriate sample containment;
- managing asbestos-contaminated disposable PPE or sampling waste;
- preventing spills or releases;
- protecting stormwater, soil, buildings, plant and client assets;
- notifying clients of observed environmental hazards;
- escalating contamination or waste issues where specialist action is required.

Environmental controls must be proportionate to Ax4's scope. Ax4 does not perform asbestos removal unless separately authorised, licensed and controlled through appropriate arrangements.

## 16. Quality and Report Defensibility

Ax4 will manage quality from initial enquiry through to final document issue.

Quality controls may include:

- clear scope definition;
- competent personnel;
- review of available background information;
- consistent field notes and photographs;
- clear distinction between observations, assumptions and limitations;
- correct use of technical terms;
- controlled report templates;
- review of reports before issue where required;
- document control and revision tracking;
- corrective action for errors or nonconforming work.

Reports must be suitable for their intended purpose and must not overstate the certainty of findings.

Where the scope is limited, the limitation must be clearly stated.

Where information is supplied by the client or a third party, reliance on that information must be identified where relevant.
