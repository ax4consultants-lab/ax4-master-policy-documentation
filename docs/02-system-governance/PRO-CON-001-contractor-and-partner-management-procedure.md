## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how Ax4 Consulting Pty Ltd selects, approves, engages, communicates with and reviews contractors, subcontractors and technical partners.

The purpose is to ensure that persons and organisations engaged by or working with Ax4 are competent, appropriately licensed where required, adequately insured, aware of relevant hazards, clear on their scope and responsibilities, and able to work in a manner consistent with Ax4’s WHS/SHEQ expectations.

This procedure supports Ax4’s asbestos, hazardous materials and WHS consulting work by controlling the use of removalists, licensed asbestos assessors, occupational hygienists, laboratories, consultants, trades, access providers and other project partners.

---

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to contractors, subcontractors and technical partners who may be engaged by Ax4, recommended by Ax4, coordinated by Ax4 or working alongside Ax4 on client projects.

This may include:

- licensed asbestos removalists;
- licensed asbestos assessors;
- occupational hygienists;
- NATA-accredited laboratories;
- hazardous materials consultants;
- air monitoring providers;
- clearance inspection providers;
- access equipment providers;
- trades or maintenance contractors;
- demolition, refurbishment or remediation contractors;
- specialist consultants engaged for technical review or project support.

This procedure applies from initial selection through to project close-out and record retention.

---

## 3. Contractor and Partner Categories

Ax4 classifies contractors and partners according to the level of risk, technical reliance and regulatory control associated with their work.

| Category | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Technical partner | Provides specialist technical advice, assessment, analysis or review | hygienist, licensed assessor, laboratory |
| Licensed contractor | Performs work requiring a licence, approval or prescribed competency | asbestos removalist, assessor, demolition contractor |
| Field support contractor | Supports site access, logistics or field work | access equipment provider, site escort, trade support |
| Administrative or professional supplier | Provides business support not normally involving site risk | insurer, legal reviewer, document consultant |
| Client-nominated contractor | Engaged by the client but working on the same project or relying on Ax4 information | builder, removalist, trade contractor |

The level of approval and evidence required depends on the type of work, risk profile, project scope and degree of reliance Ax4 or the client will place on the contractor’s work.

---

## 4. Contractor Approval Process

Before a contractor or partner is used for Ax4-controlled work, Ax4 must determine whether they are suitable for the intended scope.

Approval may include review of:

- business name and ABN;
- role and scope of work;
- relevant licences;
- insurances;
- competencies and qualifications;
- relevant experience;
- capacity to meet project requirements;
- WHS documentation such as SWMS, JSA or procedures;
- conflict of interest or independence issues;
- prior performance, incidents or client feedback.

For low-risk administrative suppliers, approval may be limited to business suitability and service quality. For asbestos, hazardous materials, removal, hygiene, clearance, sampling, analysis or high-risk site work, approval must be more rigorous.

Ax4 may maintain an approved contractor and partner list or equivalent register.

---

## 5. Licence Checks

Where a contractor or partner performs work that requires a licence, Ax4 must confirm that the licence is current and suitable for the work.

Licence checks may apply to:

- asbestos removal work;
- asbestos assessor work;
- demolition work;
- high-risk work licences;
- plant operation;
- electrical work;
- other regulated or licensed activities.

Licence checks should confirm:

- licence holder name;
- licence number;
- issuing authority;
- class or type of licence;
- expiry date;
- scope limitations or conditions;
- whether the licence covers the intended work.

A contractor must not be used for licensed work where Ax4 knows, or reasonably should know, that the contractor does not hold the required licence or the licence is not suitable for the task.

---

## 6. Insurance Checks

Contractors and partners must hold insurance appropriate to their work and risk profile.

Insurance checks may include:

- public liability insurance;
- professional indemnity insurance;
- workers compensation or equivalent cover;
- motor vehicle or plant insurance where relevant;
- asbestos-specific cover where available and relevant to the work;
- any client-required insurance levels.

Insurance evidence should be current and retained where the contractor performs technical, high-risk or client-facing work on behalf of Ax4.

Where a contractor is engaged directly by the client, Ax4 may recommend that the client verify insurance before work proceeds.

---

## 7. Competency Checks

Ax4 must take reasonable steps to confirm that contractors and partners are competent for the work they are asked to perform.

Competency evidence may include:

- licences;
- qualifications;
- training certificates;
- professional memberships;
- relevant project history;
- references or prior performance;
- sample reports or technical outputs;
- internal approval by a Director or competent technical reviewer.

Competency checks are particularly important where Ax4 relies on a contractor or partner for technical outputs such as air monitoring, clearance inspections, laboratory analysis, asbestos assessments, hazardous materials assessments or report content.

---

## 8. NATA Laboratory Control

Where laboratory analysis is required, Ax4 should use laboratories that are appropriately accredited for the relevant test method.

Laboratory controls should include:

- confirming NATA accreditation or equivalent suitability for the required analysis;
- confirming sample submission requirements;
- maintaining chain of custody or sample submission records where applicable;
- ensuring results are linked to the correct project, site, sample ID and report;
- retaining laboratory reports as controlled project records;
- not altering laboratory results.

Where a laboratory result is unclear, inconsistent or appears unsuitable for the scope, Ax4 must seek clarification before relying on it in a client report.

---

## 9. Induction Requirements

Contractors and partners may require induction depending on the site, project and role.

Induction may include:

- client or site induction;
- Ax4 project briefing;
- scope of work and boundaries;
- known hazards and risk controls;
- asbestos register or hazardous materials register review;
- emergency procedures;
- PPE/RPE expectations;
- access restrictions;
- reporting and communication requirements;
- incident and near miss reporting obligations.

For high-risk site work, evidence of induction or sign-on should be retained where practicable.

---

## 10. SWMS, JSA and Safety Documentation Review

Where contractors perform high-risk construction work, asbestos removal, intrusive work, access work, demolition, remediation or other higher-risk site activity, Ax4 or the relevant duty holder should request appropriate safety documentation before work begins.

This may include:

- SWMS;
- JSA;
- asbestos removal control plan;
- emergency procedure;
- permit to work;
- traffic management plan;
- fall prevention or access plan;
- confined space documentation;
- PPE/RPE procedures;
- waste and decontamination process.

Ax4’s review of contractor documentation is not a guarantee that the contractor’s work is safe or compliant. Contractors remain responsible for their own work, workers, methods, controls and legal obligations.

Where Ax4 identifies an obvious gap, inconsistency or conflict with known site conditions, Ax4 should raise the matter with the contractor, client or relevant duty holder before work proceeds.

---

## 11. Scope and Responsibility Boundaries

Before work begins, Ax4 should clarify the scope and responsibility boundaries between Ax4, the client and any contractor or partner.

This may include confirming:

- who engaged the contractor;
- who controls the work area;
- who is responsible for permits and access;
- who is responsible for isolation, removal, remediation or reinstatement;
- who is responsible for air monitoring or clearance;
- who receives and acts on laboratory results;
- who communicates with occupants, trades or site management;
- what Ax4 is and is not responsible for.

Ax4 must avoid wording or conduct that unintentionally transfers client duty-holder obligations, principal contractor duties or contractor work control responsibilities to Ax4 unless expressly agreed in writing and within Ax4’s lawful and competent scope.

---

## 12. Supervision and Communication

The level of supervision or coordination required depends on the project role and risk.

Ax4 may communicate with contractors and partners through:

- written scope documents;
- email instructions or confirmations;
- site briefings;
- project meetings;
- phone calls followed by written confirmation where required;
- shared site documentation;
- report appendices or project close-out packs.

Where Ax4 coordinates multiple parties, communication should be clear, factual and recorded where decisions affect safety, scope, cost, timing, contamination control or report conclusions.

Ax4 does not supervise a contractor’s work unless supervision has been expressly agreed and Ax4 is competent and authorised to perform that role.

---

## 13. Incident Reporting Obligations

Contractors and partners must report incidents, hazards and near misses that occur during work connected with Ax4 projects.

Reportable matters may include:

- injury or illness;
- suspected exposure to asbestos, hazardous materials or contaminated dust;
- uncontrolled asbestos disturbance;
- failed isolation or containment;
- unexpected hazardous material discovery;
- spill or environmental release;
- unsafe access, plant, electrical, traffic, height or confined space issue;
- failure of PPE, RPE, SWMS, JSA or field controls;
- client or contractor activity that creates an uncontrolled risk.

Where Ax4 becomes aware of an incident, Ax4 must record relevant details and escalate to the client, site controller, contractor, regulator or emergency services where required.

Contractors remain responsible for reporting incidents to regulators where their legal duties require it.

---

## 14. Specific Controls — Licensed Asbestos Removalists

Where asbestos removal work is required, Ax4 should recommend or coordinate appropriately licensed asbestos removalists where relevant to the scope.

Controls may include verifying:

- asbestos removal licence type and currency;
- suitability for friable or non-friable asbestos removal as required;
- insurance suitability;
- asbestos removal control plan where required;
- SWMS and site-specific controls;
- waste disposal arrangements;
- clearance and air monitoring requirements;
- communication with the client or person with management or control.

Ax4 does not perform asbestos removal unless separately authorised, licensed and competent to do so. Ax4 must maintain clear separation between consulting, inspection, assessment, monitoring, clearance and removal roles where independence is required.

---

## 15. Specific Controls — Licensed Asbestos Assessors and Hygienists

Where licensed asbestos assessors or occupational hygienists are engaged or recommended, Ax4 should confirm that their role matches the project requirement.

Controls may include verifying:

- licence or competency status;
- scope of assessment, air monitoring or clearance work;
- independence requirements;
- reporting format and issue timing;
- sampling or monitoring method;
- communication of urgent results;
- evidence required for project close-out.

Where Ax4 relies on assessor or hygienist outputs, those outputs should be retained with the project record and referenced accurately in Ax4 reporting where applicable.

---

## 16. Specific Controls — Laboratories

Where a laboratory is used for asbestos or hazardous materials analysis, Ax4 must ensure sample records and laboratory results are managed accurately.

Controls may include:

- sample ID consistency;
- location and material description consistency;
- secure sample packaging and transport;
- chain of custody or sample submission record;
- laboratory report review;
- linking results to the correct register, report or project file;
- retaining original laboratory reports.

Laboratory results must not be misrepresented. Where a material is not sampled or not analysed, Ax4 must not imply laboratory confirmation.

---

## 17. Specific Controls — Consultants and Technical Reviewers

Where Ax4 uses external consultants or technical reviewers, the scope of review must be clear.

This may include:

- document review;
- technical asbestos review;
- WHS system review;
- environmental review;
- legal or insurance review;
- specialist advice for unusual site conditions.

External advice must be recorded where it materially affects a report, procedure, recommendation or client instruction.

---

## 18. Client-Nominated Contractors

Where a client nominates or directly engages a contractor, Ax4 should clarify that the contractor is not controlled by Ax4 unless expressly agreed.

Ax4 may still need to communicate known hazards, scope limitations, register information or risk concerns to the client or contractor where relevant.

If a client-nominated contractor creates a risk or requests work outside Ax4’s scope, Ax4 workers may stop work, escalate the issue and record the matter.

---

## 19. Records to Retain

Relevant contractor and partner records may include:

- approved contractor register entry;
- licences;
- insurance certificates;
- training or competency evidence;
- SWMS/JSA or safety documentation;
- induction/sign-on records;
- asbestos removal control plan;
- air monitoring reports;
- clearance certificates;
- laboratory reports;
- waste disposal records;
- incident or hazard records;
- correspondence confirming scope, role or responsibility boundaries.

Records must be retained in the relevant project file, controlled register or approved business record location.

---

## 20. Performance Review

Ax4 may review contractor and partner performance based on:

- safety performance;
- incident or near miss history;
- quality of work;
- technical reliability;
- communication;
- timeliness;
- client feedback;
- completeness of records;
- compliance with agreed scope and site requirements.

Contractors or partners may be removed from preferred use where performance is unsafe, unreliable, non-compliant or inconsistent with Ax4’s standards.

## 22. Assumptions and Open Review Items

### Assumptions

- Ax4 generally acts as a consultant, coordinator or technical adviser, not as an asbestos removal contractor.
- Some contractors may be engaged directly by the client rather than by Ax4.
- Detailed project-specific requirements will be captured in quotes, scopes, SHEP/JSA/SWMS documents or project correspondence.
- Contractor approval requirements should be proportionate to the risk and technical reliance involved.
