## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how Ax4 Consulting Pty Ltd, trading as Ax4, Ax4 Asbestos Consultants and Ax4 WHS Consultants, identifies, reports, escalates, investigates, records and closes out workplace incidents, hazards and near misses.

The purpose of this procedure is to ensure that:

- immediate risks are controlled;
- injured or affected persons receive appropriate assistance;
- incidents, hazards and near misses are reported promptly;
- notifiable incident obligations are considered and escalated;
- relevant clients, site contacts, contractors and duty holders are informed where required;
- corrective actions are assigned, tracked and closed;
- records are retained as part of Ax4's WHS/SHEQ system;
- lessons learned are fed back into policies, procedures, training, templates and field controls.

This procedure supports Ax4's Work Health and Safety Policy, Environmental Policy, Quality Policy and SHEQ Management Manual.

---

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all Ax4-controlled work, including:

- asbestos inspections;
- asbestos register and management plan work;
- asbestos register reviews;
- pre-refurbishment and pre-demolition advice;
- hazardous materials assessments;
- WHS consulting activities;
- site visits, inspections, sampling activities and field assessments;
- contractor liaison and coordination activities;
- office-based, travel-related and client-facing work;
- work performed by Ax4 directors, workers, contractors, consultants and technical partners acting on behalf of Ax4.

This procedure applies to incidents, hazards and near misses involving Ax4 personnel, contractors, clients, occupants, visitors, members of the public or other persons who may be affected by Ax4's work.

Where Ax4 is working under a client-controlled site system, this procedure must be applied alongside the client's site incident, emergency, permit and reporting requirements.

This procedure does not replace legal advice. Where an incident may be notifiable, serious, disputed, legally sensitive or regulator-facing, the matter must be escalated to an Ax4 Director immediately.

## 4. Definitions

### 4.1 Incident

An incident is an unplanned event arising out of work that has caused injury, illness, exposure, damage, loss, contamination, environmental harm or interruption to safe work.

Examples include:

- injury or illness;
- suspected exposure to asbestos, lead, silica, mould, synthetic mineral fibre or other hazardous materials;
- asbestos disturbance or uncontrolled asbestos-containing dust or debris;
- fall, slip, trip or manual handling injury;
- electric shock, contact with live services or uncontrolled energy source;
- traffic, plant, access or structural event;
- uncontrolled release, spill or contamination;
- fire, explosion or emergency event;
- loss or failure of critical PPE, RPE, equipment or controls.

### 4.2 Hazard

A hazard is a source or situation with the potential to cause harm to people, property, the environment, client operations or the integrity of Ax4's work.

Examples include:

- damaged asbestos-containing material;
- suspected asbestos-containing dust or debris;
- unsafe roof space or ceiling access;
- fragile surfaces;
- exposed electrical services;
- poor lighting or restricted access;
- uncontrolled contractor activity;
- inadequate site information;
- missing asbestos register or site induction;
- aggressive behaviour or psychosocial risk;
- missing PPE/RPE or unsuitable equipment.

### 4.3 Near Miss

A near miss is an event or condition that did not result in injury, illness, exposure, damage or contamination, but had the potential to do so.

Near misses must be reported because they provide evidence of controls that may be weak, missing or unreliable.

### 4.4 Notifiable Incident

A notifiable incident is a workplace event that must be reported to the WHS regulator. Under WHS laws, this generally includes:

- the death of a person;
- a serious injury or illness;
- a dangerous incident that exposes a person to a serious risk, even if no injury occurs.

Potential notifiable incidents must be escalated to an Ax4 Director immediately.

### 4.5 Corrective Action

A corrective action is an action taken to remove, reduce or control the cause of an incident, hazard, near miss or system weakness.

### 4.6 Close-Out

Close-out means the issue has been reviewed, required actions have been completed or formally transferred, records have been updated and any required communication has occurred.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

### 5.1 Directors

Ax4 Directors are responsible for:

- ensuring this procedure is implemented;
- reviewing serious incidents, potential notifiable incidents and regulator-facing matters;
- deciding whether legal, technical, regulatory or client escalation is required;
- ensuring notifiable incident obligations are considered promptly;
- approving significant corrective actions;
- ensuring resources are available to control risks and complete investigations;
- reviewing trends and lessons learned during management review.

### 5.2 Managers, Supervisors and Project Leads

Managers, supervisors and persons coordinating Ax4 work are responsible for:

- ensuring incidents, hazards and near misses are reported and recorded;
- making sure immediate controls are implemented;
- notifying Ax4 Directors where escalation triggers are met;
- liaising with clients, site contacts and contractors where required;
- assigning corrective actions;
- verifying action close-out;
- ensuring relevant project records are retained.

### 5.3 Workers, Consultants and Contractors

Workers, consultants and contractors undertaking work for Ax4 must:

- take reasonable care for their own health and safety;
- take reasonable care that their acts or omissions do not adversely affect others;
- stop work where there is an uncontrolled or unacceptable risk;
- report incidents, hazards and near misses as soon as practicable;
- assist with immediate controls where safe to do so;
- preserve incident evidence where required and safe;
- provide accurate information for incident records;
- cooperate with investigation and corrective action processes.

### 5.4 Clients and Site Contacts

Clients and site contacts may have their own duties as a PCBU, person with management or control of a workplace, property owner, principal contractor, employer or other duty holder.

Ax4 must not assume that it controls the client's duty-holder obligations unless expressly agreed in writing and within Ax4's lawful scope of service.

Where an incident, hazard or near miss involves a client-controlled site, Ax4 must notify the relevant client or site contact as appropriate and cooperate with their site procedures.

## 6. Reporting Triggers

The following must be reported under this procedure:

- any injury, illness or suspected exposure;
- any asbestos disturbance, suspected asbestos exposure or uncontrolled asbestos-containing dust or debris;
- any uncontrolled hazardous material, contamination or environmental release;
- any event requiring first aid, medical treatment or emergency services;
- any dangerous incident or serious near miss;
- any unsafe access, fragile surface, fall risk, roof space or ceiling space concern;
- any electric shock, contact with live services or uncontrolled energy source;
- any traffic, plant, confined space, excavation, height or structural risk;
- any client, contractor or third-party activity that creates risk to Ax4 workers or others;
- any instruction from a client or contractor that appears unsafe or outside agreed scope;
- any failure of PPE, RPE, equipment, permit systems or controls;
- any condition that causes work to stop, pause or change.

Where a worker is unsure whether something should be reported, they must report it.

## 7. Immediate Response

Where an incident, hazard or near miss occurs, the first priority is to protect people from further harm.

Immediate response steps are:

1. Stop work where required.
2. Move to a safe location if safe to do so.
3. Provide or arrange first aid if required.
4. Contact emergency services on 000 where there is immediate danger, serious injury, fire, collapse, uncontrolled exposure, serious contamination or other emergency.
5. Prevent further access or disturbance where safe and practicable.
6. Notify the Ax4 Director, manager or project lead.
7. Notify the client or site contact where the matter affects the site, occupants, contractors or client operations.
8. Preserve the scene where a notifiable incident may have occurred, except where disturbance is necessary to assist an injured person, remove a deceased person, make the site safe or prevent further incident.
9. Record key facts, photographs and names of persons involved where safe and appropriate.
10. Do not recommence work until the risk has been reassessed and authorisation has been given.

## 8. Asbestos and Hazardous Materials Response

If asbestos-containing material, suspected asbestos-containing material, asbestos-contaminated dust or debris, lead-containing materials, synthetic mineral fibre, mould-affected material or other hazardous material is disturbed or unexpectedly identified, Ax4 personnel must:

- stop work immediately;
- avoid further disturbance;
- prevent access to the affected area where safe and practicable;
- avoid tracking dust, debris or contamination from the affected area;
- use suitable PPE/RPE based on the risk and only if safe to do so;
- notify the Ax4 Director or project lead;
- notify the client or site contact;
- record the location, material, condition, photographs and circumstances;
- recommend further assessment, sampling, isolation, decontamination, licensed removal, hygienist support, air monitoring or clearance where required;
- not represent the area as safe for unrestricted access unless this is within Ax4's scope and supported by appropriate evidence.

Where there is suspected airborne fibre release, contamination spread, uncontrolled disturbance or potential exposure, the matter must be escalated as a serious incident.

Ax4 must not state that a site or area is asbestos-free. Any advice must be limited to the agreed scope, available evidence, accessible areas, sampling status and known limitations.

## 9. Notifiable Incident Escalation

A potential notifiable incident must be escalated immediately to an Ax4 Director.

Potential notifiable incident indicators include:

- death of a person;
- serious injury or illness;
- hospital treatment or urgent medical treatment;
- serious exposure or suspected exposure to asbestos, hazardous substances or airborne contaminants;
- electric shock or uncontrolled electrical incident;
- collapse, partial collapse or structural failure;
- fall from height or falling object with serious risk;
- uncontrolled fire, explosion, spill, pressure release or dangerous substance event;
- any dangerous incident that exposes a person to serious risk, even if no injury occurs.

If the incident is life-threatening or involves a death, emergency services must be contacted immediately and SafeWork SA's emergency incident notification pathway must be considered.

A person conducting a business or undertaking must notify SafeWork SA as soon as practicable after becoming aware of a notifiable incident. Where the incident occurs on a client-controlled site, Ax4 must cooperate with the client or controlling PCBU but must also ensure Ax4's own obligations are considered.

The site of a potential notifiable incident must not be disturbed unless necessary to:

- assist an injured person;
- remove a deceased person;
- make the site safe;
- prevent further incident;
- comply with a direction from emergency services, police, SafeWork SA or another authorised person.

## 10. Internal Reporting Timeframes

The following internal timeframes apply:

| Event Type | Internal Reporting Timeframe |
|---|---|
| Life-threatening incident, death, serious injury, serious exposure or emergency | Immediately by phone to an Ax4 Director |
| Potential notifiable incident | Immediately by phone to an Ax4 Director |
| Asbestos disturbance, suspected exposure or uncontrolled hazardous material | Immediately by phone to an Ax4 Director or project lead |
| Injury requiring first aid or medical attention | Same day |
| Hazard requiring work to stop or change | Immediately before work continues |
| Near miss with serious potential | Same day |
| Minor hazard or improvement item | As soon as practicable and no later than the next working day |

Phone notification should be followed by written record as soon as practicable.

## 11. Incident, Hazard and Near Miss Record

Records must be clear, factual and suitable for internal review, client communication and regulator-facing use where required.

Records should include:

- date and time;
- location and project reference;
- person reporting;
- persons involved or affected;
- client/site contact notified;
- description of what occurred or what was observed;
- task being performed;
- immediate controls applied;
- injury, illness, exposure, contamination or damage details;
- photographs or supporting evidence where appropriate;
- whether work was stopped;
- whether emergency services, client, contractor or regulator notification was required;
- initial risk rating or seriousness classification;
- corrective actions required;
- responsible person;
- due date;
- close-out verification.

Records must avoid speculation, blame or unsupported conclusions. Where facts are uncertain, the record should state that the matter is under review.

---

## 12. Investigation Process

The level of investigation must be proportionate to the seriousness, risk and potential consequences of the event.

Investigation may include:

- securing immediate evidence;
- speaking with workers, contractors, clients or witnesses;
- reviewing site conditions;
- reviewing photographs, field notes, registers, reports and access limitations;
- reviewing SHEP, JSA, SWMS, permits, inductions and training records;
- reviewing PPE/RPE and equipment condition;
- identifying direct causes and contributing factors;
- identifying whether the issue arose from planning, communication, competency, access, client information, contractor interface, procedure, equipment or changed site conditions;
- identifying corrective and preventive actions.

Investigations must focus on preventing recurrence, not assigning blame.

Serious incidents, suspected asbestos exposure, potential notifiable incidents and regulator-facing matters must be reviewed by an Ax4 Director.

---

## 13. Corrective Actions

Corrective actions must be assigned to a responsible person and tracked until close-out.

Corrective actions may include:

- stopping or rescheduling work;
- updating scope or access requirements;
- isolating or restricting access to an area;
- notifying the client or duty holder;
- arranging further assessment, sampling, removal, decontamination, clearance or specialist review;
- revising SHEP, JSA, SWMS or field controls;
- updating a procedure, checklist, form or template;
- providing additional training, supervision or briefing;
- replacing or improving PPE, RPE, tools or equipment;
- updating a register, report, limitation statement or project record;
- reviewing contractor performance;
- escalating to legal, insurance, technical or regulator advice where required.

Corrective actions must be proportionate to the risk and must not create unsupported guarantees or transfer client duties to Ax4 unless expressly agreed.

---

## 14. Close-Out Requirements

An incident, hazard or near miss may only be closed when:

- immediate risks have been controlled;
- affected persons have been assisted where required;
- required notifications have been made or considered;
- records have been completed;
- corrective actions have been assigned and completed or formally transferred;
- client or site communication has occurred where required;
- project files have been updated;
- any required review by an Ax4 Director has occurred;
- lessons learned have been identified where relevant.

Where the corrective action is controlled by the client, principal contractor, removalist, hygienist or other duty holder, Ax4 must record the transfer of action and retain evidence of the communication. Ax4 should not mark a client-controlled risk as resolved unless there is adequate evidence within Ax4's scope.

---

## 15. Communication and Confidentiality

Incident communication must be factual, controlled and appropriate to the audience.

Workers must not provide speculative, legal, medical or liability-based opinions to clients, contractors, occupants or third parties.

External communication should be managed by an Ax4 Director or nominated project lead where the matter involves:

- serious injury or illness;
- suspected exposure;
- asbestos disturbance or contamination;
- potential regulator notification;
- media, legal, insurance or contractual sensitivity;
- client dispute or complaint;
- reputational risk.

Project communications must be retained in the project file where they form part of the evidence trail.

---

## 16. Records and Retention

Incident, hazard and near miss records must be retained in the relevant Ax4 WHS/SHEQ or project record system.

Records may include:

- incident/hazard/near miss forms;
- photographs;
- emails and client notifications;
- field notes;
- SHEP, JSA, SWMS and induction records;
- training records;
- laboratory reports;
- asbestos registers or hazardous materials registers;
- clearance certificates, ARCPs, air monitoring results or removal documentation where relevant;
- regulator notifications and correspondence;
- corrective action records;
- close-out evidence.

Records must be stored so they are retrievable, controlled and protected from inappropriate alteration.

---

## 17. Review and Continuous Improvement

Incident, hazard and near miss information must be used to improve Ax4's WHS/SHEQ system.

Review activities may include:

- management review of incident trends;
- review of repeated hazards or near misses;
- review of high-risk work categories;
- review of asbestos disturbance or exposure controls;
- update of SHEP, JSA, SWMS, SOPs or templates;
- update of training and competency requirements;
- review of contractor and partner performance;
- review of report limitations and client communication wording;
- review of emergency response arrangements.

This procedure must be reviewed:

- at least every two years;
- following a significant incident, exposure or near miss;
- following relevant legislative, regulator or code changes;
- following a system audit or Director review finding;
- where operational experience identifies a need for improvement.
