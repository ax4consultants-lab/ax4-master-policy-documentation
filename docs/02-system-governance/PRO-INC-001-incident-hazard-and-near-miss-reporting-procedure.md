## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how Ax4 Consulting Pty Ltd identifies, reports, records, investigates, escalates and closes out incidents, hazards and near misses.

The purpose of this procedure is to ensure that incidents and hazards are managed promptly, consistently and defensibly, and that corrective actions are implemented to prevent recurrence.

This procedure supports Ax4’s Work Health and Safety Policy, Environmental Policy, Quality Policy, SHEQ Management Manual and site-specific field controls.

---

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- Ax4 directors, workers, contractors, consultants and subcontractors;
- field work, office work, travel, client meetings, site inspections, sampling activities, report preparation and contractor coordination;
- asbestos, hazardous materials, WHS consulting and related compliance work;
- incidents, hazards, near misses, unsafe conditions, environmental events, quality events and suspected exposures arising from or identified during Ax4 work.

This procedure applies whether the event occurs at an Ax4-controlled workplace, a client site, a residential property, commercial property, school, aged care facility, government site, hospitality site, industrial site, construction site or other workplace attended by Ax4.

---

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Incident

An incident is an event that caused or could have caused injury, illness, exposure, property damage, environmental harm, contamination, loss, nonconforming work or interruption to safe work.

### 3.2 Hazard

A hazard is a situation, source or condition with the potential to cause harm to people, property, the environment, report integrity or legal compliance.

### 3.3 Near Miss

A near miss is an event that did not cause harm but had the potential to cause harm if circumstances had been slightly different.

### 3.4 Notifiable Incident

A notifiable incident is an incident that must be notified to SafeWork SA under applicable South Australian WHS requirements. This may include a fatality, serious injury or illness, or dangerous incident arising out of the conduct of the business or undertaking.

### 3.5 Corrective Action

Corrective action is action taken to eliminate the cause of an incident, hazard, near miss or nonconformance and prevent recurrence.

### 3.6 Preventive Action

Preventive action is action taken to reduce the likelihood of a potential issue occurring.

## 4. Reportable Event Categories

Ax4 workers and contractors must report the following as soon as practicable.

### 4.1 Injury or Illness

Examples include:

- cuts, bruises, sprains, strains, slips, trips or falls;
- heat stress, fatigue, dizziness or illness at work;
- respiratory irritation, eye irritation or skin irritation;
- medical treatment or first aid treatment;
- injury to a worker, contractor, client, occupant, visitor or member of the public.

### 4.2 Suspected Exposure

Examples include:

- suspected asbestos fibre exposure;
- suspected contact with asbestos-contaminated dust or debris;
- suspected lead, mould, synthetic mineral fibre or chemical exposure;
- failure of PPE, RPE or decontamination controls;
- entry into an uncontrolled or contaminated area.

### 4.3 Dangerous or High-Risk Event

Examples include:

- electrical shock, electrical fault or contact with live services;
- fall from height or fall through fragile material;
- collapse, instability or structural concern;
- confined or restricted space concern;
- vehicle or traffic interaction;
- uncontrolled asbestos disturbance;
- uncontrolled dust, debris or contamination release;
- failure of critical controls.

### 4.4 Environmental Event

Examples include:

- release or spread of asbestos-containing dust, debris or contaminated material;
- spill to soil, drain, stormwater, building area or sensitive environment;
- incorrect waste handling or suspected incorrect waste disposal;
- damage that creates contamination risk;
- uncontrolled tracking of dust or debris.

### 4.5 Quality or Compliance Event

Examples include:

- incorrect client, site or project details in a controlled document;
- missing sample result, photo, appendix or register item;
- incorrect or unsupported technical statement;
- unclear scope, limitation or exclusion;
- use of an outdated template;
- report issue error;
- client complaint or significant request for correction;
- failure to follow a required procedure, JSA, SWMS or site requirement.

### 4.6 Unsafe Condition or Hazard

Examples include:

- unsafe access or unstable surfaces;
- poorly lit or restricted work areas;
- exposed electrical components;
- uncontrolled asbestos or suspected hazardous materials;
- aggressive animals, pests or biological hazards;
- unsafe contractor activities;
- missing site information, missing induction or inadequate controls.

## 5. Immediate Response

Where an incident, hazard or near miss occurs, the person involved or observing the issue must:

1. stop work if safe to do so;
2. make the area safe, only if it is safe and within their competence to do so;
3. protect people from immediate harm;
4. seek first aid or emergency assistance where required;
5. isolate or restrict access to affected areas where appropriate;
6. notify the Ax4 Director or nominated project lead as soon as practicable;
7. notify the client, site contact or principal contractor where site control, access, emergency response or client duty-holder action is required;
8. preserve the incident scene where the event may be notifiable or requires investigation, except where action is needed to assist an injured person, make the area safe or comply with lawful directions;
9. record the event using Ax4’s incident, hazard or near miss reporting process.

Where there is immediate danger to life, serious injury, fire, explosion or another emergency, emergency services must be contacted by calling 000.

## 6. Notification and Escalation

### 6.1 Internal Notification

All incidents, hazards and near misses must be reported internally as soon as practicable.

Immediate verbal notification is required for:

- injury or illness requiring medical treatment;
- suspected asbestos, lead, mould or hazardous materials exposure;
- uncontrolled asbestos disturbance or contamination release;
- electrical shock or live service incident;
- fall, collapse, structural concern or confined space concern;
- event involving a client, occupant, contractor, visitor or member of the public;
- event that may be notifiable to SafeWork SA;
- event requiring work to stop.

### 6.2 Client or Site Notification

The client, site contact, principal contractor or person with management or control of the workplace must be notified where:

- the event affects their site, occupants, contractors or operations;
- access restriction, isolation, repair, cleaning, removal or specialist response is required;
- an asbestos or hazardous materials risk is identified;
- a duty-holder decision is required before work can continue;
- incident records or evidence must be shared to support site management.

Ax4 notification to a client does not transfer Ax4 into the role of principal contractor, site controller, asbestos removalist or client duty holder unless expressly agreed in writing and legally appropriate.

### 6.3 SafeWork SA Notification

The Director or nominated competent person must assess whether an event is a notifiable incident under South Australian WHS requirements.

Where an incident is notifiable, SafeWork SA must be notified as soon as practicable after Ax4 becomes aware of the incident.

Notifiable incidents may include fatalities, serious injuries or illnesses and dangerous incidents arising out of the conduct of the business or undertaking.

For life-threatening matters or a fatality, SafeWork SA should be contacted using the applicable emergency notification channel. For non-life-threatening notifiable matters, the applicable SafeWork SA notification process must be followed.

The incident site must be preserved where legally required, except where disturbance is necessary to assist an injured person, remove a deceased person, make the site safe, support a police investigation or comply with inspector directions.

### 6.4 Other Regulatory or External Notification

Other notification may be required depending on the event, including notification to:

- emergency services;
- the client or site controller;
- the principal contractor;
- EPA South Australia for environmental matters where applicable;
- an insurer;
- a licensed asbestos removalist, occupational hygienist, licensed asbestos assessor or laboratory;
- other duty holders affected by the event.

The Director will determine external notification requirements based on the nature of the event and applicable legal, contractual and insurance obligations.

## 7. Incident and Hazard Reporting Records

Incident, hazard and near miss records should include, where relevant:

- date and time of event;
- date and time reported;
- person reporting;
- persons involved;
- site address and exact location;
- client or project reference;
- description of what happened;
- activity being performed at the time;
- known or suspected hazards involved;
- injury, illness, exposure, damage or contamination details;
- immediate controls applied;
- whether work was stopped;
- whether emergency services, client, site contact or regulator was notified;
- photographs, sketches or supporting evidence where appropriate;
- witnesses;
- initial risk rating or seriousness assessment;
- corrective actions required;
- person responsible for each action;
- due date;
- close-out evidence;
- final review and sign-off.

Records must be factual, timely, clear and free from unsupported conclusions.

## 8. Investigation Process

The level of investigation must be proportionate to the actual or potential severity of the event.

### 8.1 Initial Review

The Director or nominated competent person must review the report and determine:

- what happened;
- who was involved or affected;
- whether anyone requires medical support;
- whether the event is notifiable;
- whether the area must remain isolated or preserved;
- whether work can safely continue;
- what immediate corrective actions are required;
- whether specialist advice is required.

### 8.2 Investigation Depth

Minor events may require a simple review and corrective action record.

Significant events require a more detailed investigation, including review of:

- scope and work instructions;
- SHEP, JSA, SWMS or procedure requirements;
- site induction and site rules;
- worker training and competency;
- PPE, RPE and equipment suitability;
- client-provided information;
- site conditions;
- contractor activity;
- communication and supervision;
- whether existing controls were adequate and implemented.

### 8.3 Cause Analysis

The investigation should identify immediate causes and underlying causes.

Underlying causes may include:

- incomplete scope or poor information;
- inadequate hazard identification;
- ineffective controls;
- lack of training or competency;
- poor communication;
- unclear responsibility allocation;
- inadequate document control;
- time pressure or access constraints;
- client or contractor actions;
- outdated templates or procedures.

---

## 9. Corrective and Preventive Actions

Corrective and preventive actions must be proportionate to the risk and must be assigned to a responsible person.

Actions may include:

- stopping work until controls are implemented;
- isolating or restricting access;
- notifying the client or duty holder;
- arranging licensed asbestos removal, hygienist support, clearance inspection or specialist review;
- revising a report, register or management plan;
- correcting a template or document control issue;
- updating a JSA, SHEP, SWMS or procedure;
- providing additional training, instruction or supervision;
- changing PPE, RPE or equipment;
- reviewing contractor performance;
- updating quote, scope or limitation wording;
- recording lessons learned.

Corrective actions must include:

- action description;
- responsible person;
- due date;
- completion evidence;
- close-out approval.

Actions must not be closed until completion has been verified.

---

## 10. Stop-Work and Recommencement

Any Ax4 worker or contractor may stop work where they believe work is unsafe, uncontrolled or outside the agreed scope.

Work must not recommence until:

- the hazard or incident has been assessed;
- immediate risks have been controlled;
- required notifications have been made;
- client, site or duty-holder approvals have been obtained where required;
- the SHEP, JSA, SWMS or work instruction has been updated where required;
- workers and contractors have been briefed on changed controls;
- the Director or nominated project lead has authorised recommencement.

No person will be penalised for stopping work in good faith because of a health, safety, environmental or compliance concern.

---

## 11. Asbestos and Hazardous Materials Events

Asbestos and hazardous materials events require careful control due to potential health, legal and client duty-holder implications.

Examples include:

- accidental disturbance of suspected or confirmed asbestos-containing material;
- discovery of asbestos-contaminated dust or debris;
- suspected exposure to asbestos fibres;
- discovery of unknown hazardous materials during inspection;
- damaged asbestos-containing materials outside the agreed scope;
- failure of containment, PPE, RPE or decontamination controls;
- uncontrolled tracking of contaminated material.

Immediate response may include:

1. stop work;
2. avoid further disturbance;
3. move people away from the affected area if safe to do so;
4. isolate or restrict access;
5. notify the Ax4 Director or project lead;
6. notify the client, site contact or duty holder;
7. retain photographs or notes only where safe and appropriate;
8. seek specialist advice where required;
9. recommend licensed removalist, hygienist, assessor, air monitoring or clearance involvement where required;
10. record the event and actions taken.

Ax4 must not perform asbestos removal or decontamination work outside its scope, licence position or competence.

---

## 12. Quality Events and Report Corrections

Quality events must be recorded where an issue may affect technical accuracy, client reliance, legal defensibility or document control.

Examples include:

- incorrect site address, client name, date or project reference;
- omitted sample result or incorrect sample reference;
- inconsistent register entry, photo, plan or recommendation;
- unclear limitation, exclusion or assumption;
- incorrect risk rating or recommended action;
- document issued using an outdated template;
- client complaint or significant clarification request.

Where a quality event affects an issued document, the Director or nominated reviewer must determine whether:

- a correction email is sufficient;
- a revised document must be issued;
- the document must be withdrawn and reissued;
- the client or third party must be notified;
- internal templates, procedures or training require update.

Revised documents must be controlled in accordance with Ax4’s document control process.

---

## 13. Records and Retention

Incident, hazard, near miss, corrective action and investigation records must be retained in the relevant project file or controlled business record location.

Records may include:

- incident or hazard report form;
- photographs;
- emails or client notifications;
- SafeWork SA notification records;
- medical or first aid records where appropriate and lawfully retained;
- investigation notes;
- corrective action records;
- revised reports or registers;
- contractor documents;
- clearance, laboratory, waste or specialist records;
- management review notes.

Records must be stored securely and managed with appropriate regard to privacy, confidentiality and legal defensibility.

## 14. Responsibilities

### 14.1 Directors / Management

Directors and management are responsible for:

- maintaining this procedure;
- ensuring incidents, hazards and near misses are reported and reviewed;
- determining notifiable incident requirements;
- ensuring regulator, client and external notifications are made where required;
- assigning and reviewing corrective actions;
- ensuring serious or repeated issues are considered during management review;
- ensuring lessons learned are applied to procedures, training and templates.

### 14.2 Project Lead / Consultant

The project lead or consultant is responsible for:

- stopping work where required;
- making immediate notifications;
- recording the event;
- securing evidence where safe and appropriate;
- assisting with investigation;
- briefing workers, contractors or clients on changed controls;
- ensuring work does not recommence until authorised.

### 14.3 Workers and Contractors

Workers and contractors are responsible for:

- reporting incidents, hazards and near misses as soon as practicable;
- taking reasonable care for their own health and safety;
- not placing others at risk;
- following Ax4 procedures, site rules and reasonable instructions;
- using PPE and RPE correctly;
- stopping work where conditions are unsafe or unclear;
- cooperating with incident investigations.

### 14.4 Client / Site Contact Interface

Where an event occurs at a client site, the relevant client or site contact is responsible for decisions and controls within their management or control, including site access, occupant notification, repairs, removal works, contractor coordination and implementation of ongoing controls unless otherwise agreed in writing.

---

## 15. Review and Lessons Learned

The Director or nominated person must review significant incidents, recurring hazards and corrective action trends.

Review outcomes may result in:

- updated SHEP, JSA or SWMS content;
- revised inspection or sampling procedures;
- updated report templates or limitation wording;
- additional worker or contractor training;
- changes to contractor approval or monitoring;
- updates to emergency response arrangements;
- changes to client communication or scope wording;
- management review actions.

This procedure will be reviewed:

- at least every two years;
- following a notifiable incident;
- following a significant incident, exposure, environmental event or quality failure;
- following changes to relevant legislation, guidance or Ax4 services;
- where audits, client feedback or operational review identifies a need for change.
