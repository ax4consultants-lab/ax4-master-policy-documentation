## 1. Purpose

Ax4 Consulting Pty Ltd, trading as Ax4, Ax4 Asbestos Consultants and Ax4 WHS Consultants, is committed to providing accurate, consistent, technically sound and legally defensible consulting services.

This Quality Policy sets out Ax4's commitment to maintaining a quality-focused system for inspection, assessment, documentation, client communication, reporting, record keeping, review and continuous improvement.

The purpose of this policy is to ensure that Ax4 work outputs are:

- accurate and evidence-based;
- prepared by competent personnel;
- aligned with agreed scope and client requirements;
- reviewed before issue where required;
- clearly documented and controlled;
- suitable for their intended purpose;
- capable of supporting legal, regulatory, WHS, client and due diligence requirements.

This policy supports Ax4's asbestos, hazardous materials, WHS consulting, environmental compliance and associated advisory services.

---

## 2. Policy Statement

Ax4 will maintain a quality management approach that supports reliable, defensible and professional consulting work.

Ax4 recognises that quality in consulting is not limited to the final report. Quality begins at first client contact and continues through scope definition, site preparation, inspection, sampling decisions, evidence capture, risk assessment, reporting, review, communication, record retention and follow-up action.

Ax4 will apply quality controls that are proportionate to the risk, complexity and intended use of each project.

Where Ax4 prepares asbestos registers, asbestos management plans, hazardous materials documentation, WHS advice, survey reports or related compliance documents, those outputs must be based on clear scope, competent judgement, appropriate evidence, controlled records and transparent limitations.

---

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to all Ax4-controlled work, including:

- asbestos registers;
- asbestos management plans;
- asbestos register reviews;
- pre-demolition and pre-refurbishment survey reports;
- hazardous materials assessments;
- WHS consulting documents;
- risk assessments;
- site-specific safety, health and environment documentation;
- client correspondence containing technical advice;
- quality-controlled templates, registers, checklists and internal procedures.

This policy applies to all Ax4 personnel and any subcontractors or consultants engaged to assist Ax4, including licensed asbestos assessors, asbestos consultants, occupational hygienists, laboratories, technical reviewers and administrative personnel handling controlled documents.

## 4. Legislative and Good Practice Alignment

Ax4's quality system will be aligned, so far as is reasonably practicable and relevant to the work scope, with applicable legal, regulatory and good practice requirements, including:

- Work Health and Safety Act 2012 (SA);
- Work Health and Safety Regulations 2012 (SA), including asbestos-related duties where applicable;
- SafeWork SA guidance;
- Safe Work Australia Codes of Practice and guidance material;
- relevant Australian Standards, where applicable to the work scope;
- client-specific site requirements, where they do not reduce legal, technical or safety obligations.

Ax4 recognises that South Australian WHS and asbestos compliance duties sit with the relevant person conducting a business or undertaking, person with management or control of the workplace, officers, workers and other duty holders as defined by law. Ax4's role is to provide competent consulting services within the agreed scope. Ax4 does not assume control of client duty-holder obligations unless expressly agreed in writing and legally appropriate.

## 5. Quality Commitments

### 5.1 Technical Accuracy

Ax4 will take reasonable steps to ensure that technical findings, conclusions, risk ratings, recommendations and report wording are accurate and supported by evidence.

Technical accuracy must be supported by, where applicable:

- appropriate site observations;
- clear photographs where practicable;
- accurate location descriptions;
- correct material identification terminology;
- sampling and laboratory analysis where required, approved and practicable;
- clear distinction between confirmed asbestos-containing material, presumed asbestos-containing material, suspected material and inaccessible areas;
- appropriate limitations and exclusions;
- review of previous reports, registers, management plans, drawings or client-supplied information where available.

Where material has not been sampled, Ax4 will not present the material as analytically confirmed unless supported by reliable laboratory evidence. Where uncertainty remains, the report must state the uncertainty clearly and use controlled wording such as suspected, presumed or visually assessed.

Ax4 will not state or imply that a site, building or work area is asbestos-free unless the scope, evidence and legal context support that statement. For asbestos consulting work, reports must recognise that visual inspections and limited inspections cannot guarantee that all asbestos-containing material will be identified, particularly where access, destructive investigation, concealed services, construction layers or client constraints limit the inspection.

### 5.2 Competent Personnel

Ax4 will ensure that work is planned, undertaken, reviewed and issued by personnel who are competent for the work they perform.

Competence may be demonstrated through:

- qualifications;
- licences;
- industry experience;
- task-specific training;
- supervision;
- demonstrated understanding of asbestos and hazardous materials risks;
- familiarity with applicable legislation, codes and guidance;
- ability to apply Ax4 templates, procedures, risk systems and document control requirements.

Personnel must not undertake technical work outside their competence unless supervised by a competent person.

Where specialist input is required, Ax4 may engage competent subcontractors, licensed asbestos assessors, occupational hygienists, NATA-accredited laboratories or other suitably qualified specialists.

### 5.3 Clear Scope Definition

Ax4 will define the scope of work clearly before work commences.

Scope definition must address, where relevant:

- client name and contact;
- site address;
- purpose of the work;
- areas included;
- areas excluded;
- type of inspection or review;
- whether the work is visual, semi-intrusive, intrusive or destructive;
- sampling expectations and limitations;
- access requirements;
- deliverables;
- assumptions;
- client-supplied information relied upon;
- known limitations;
- site-specific safety or operational constraints.

Ax4 will clearly communicate where a requested scope may be insufficient for the client's stated objective. Where additional investigation, sampling, intrusive inspection, clearance documentation or specialist review is required, Ax4 will advise the client as soon as reasonably practicable.

### 5.4 Report Review and Quality Assurance

Ax4 will apply review and quality assurance controls before reports or controlled documents are issued.

The level of review will be proportionate to the complexity and risk of the work.

Report review may include checks for:

- correct client and site details;
- correct scope and purpose;
- consistency between site notes, photographs, sample results, register entries and recommendations;
- correct use of risk ratings and action priorities;
- accurate legislative and guidance references;
- correct document version and date;
- clear limitations;
- clear recommendations;
- typographical and formatting errors;
- suitability for client use;
- internal consistency between register, management plan, appendices, drawings, photos and attachments.

Where a technical review identifies an error, omission, inconsistency or uncertainty, the document must not be issued until the issue is resolved or appropriately qualified.

### 5.5 Client Communication

Ax4 will communicate with clients in a clear, professional, timely and technically responsible manner.

Client communication must support quality outcomes by ensuring that:

- scope is understood before work begins;
- access requirements are explained;
- sampling limitations are understood;
- urgent risks are escalated promptly;
- assumptions and limitations are clearly explained;
- clients understand what a report can and cannot be relied upon for;
- changes to scope, timing or deliverables are documented;
- requests for clarification are answered accurately;
- technical statements are not overstated.

Where Ax4 identifies an issue that may affect safety, legal compliance, project timing or the reliability of a report, Ax4 will communicate the issue to the client or relevant duty holder as soon as reasonably practicable.

### 5.6 Document Control

Ax4 will maintain document control processes to ensure that controlled documents are current, identifiable, traceable and protected from unauthorised or unintended alteration.

Document control must include, where applicable:

- document title;
- document code;
- version or revision number;
- date of issue;
- author or preparer;
- reviewer or approver;
- project reference;
- client reference;
- status, such as draft, issued, superseded or final;
- controlled file storage location;
- retained copies of issued documents;
- record of significant amendments.

Superseded templates, reports, registers and policies must be removed from active use or clearly marked as superseded.

Where Ax4 issues revised reports or registers, the reason for revision should be documented where practicable.

### 5.7 Corrective Action

Ax4 will maintain a corrective action process to respond to errors, complaints, nonconforming work, audit findings, client concerns or identified system weaknesses.

Corrective action may be triggered by:

- report error;
- incorrect client or site information;
- technical inconsistency;
- missed attachment, photo, sample result or register entry;
- unclear scope;
- client complaint;
- internal review finding;
- site incident;
- subcontractor performance issue;
- outdated template;
- regulatory or legal change;
- recurring administrative or technical issue.

Corrective action must be proportionate to risk and may include:

- immediate correction;
- revised report issue;
- client notification;
- root cause review;
- template amendment;
- worker or contractor briefing;
- additional training;
- subcontractor review;
- additional technical review;
- update to a procedure, checklist or register.

Significant corrective actions must be recorded and retained.

### 5.8 Continuous Improvement

Ax4 will continually improve its quality system through review, feedback, lessons learned, corrective actions, template refinement and monitoring of regulatory or industry changes.

Continuous improvement may include:

- updating report templates;
- refining inspection checklists;
- improving register wording;
- standardising recommendation codes;
- reviewing client feedback;
- improving digital workflows;
- reviewing changes in legislation, codes and standards;
- improving document control systems;
- reviewing subcontractor performance;
- improving training and competency records;
- improving automation and report-generation controls.

### 5.9 Legally Defensible Reporting

Ax4 is committed to preparing reports and technical documents that are suitable for legal, WHS, compliance, conveyancing, project and due diligence contexts, within the agreed scope and limitations of the engagement.

Legally defensible reporting requires that Ax4 reports:

- accurately reflect the agreed scope;
- distinguish facts from assumptions;
- identify limitations;
- identify inaccessible or excluded areas;
- avoid unsupported conclusions;
- retain evidence supporting findings;
- state reliance on client-provided information where relevant;
- include clear risk ratings and recommended actions;
- avoid declaring a property, area or material asbestos-free unless the scope and evidence legally and technically support that statement;
- clearly document when materials are suspected, presumed or require further investigation;
- align with relevant legal, regulatory and industry guidance.

## 6. Responsibilities

### 6.1 Directors / Senior Management

Directors and senior management are responsible for:

- approving this policy;
- ensuring sufficient resources are available to support quality work;
- ensuring competent personnel are engaged;
- reviewing significant quality issues;
- ensuring corrective actions are implemented;
- promoting a culture of accuracy, defensibility and continual improvement.

### 6.2 Project Lead / Consultant

The project lead or consultant is responsible for:

- confirming the scope;
- planning the work;
- identifying information required from the client;
- ensuring observations and evidence are recorded;
- identifying limitations;
- preparing technically accurate documents;
- escalating issues requiring review or client clarification;
- ensuring reports are not issued before required quality checks are complete.

### 6.3 Reviewer / Approver

The reviewer or approver is responsible for:

- reviewing the report or document against the agreed scope;
- checking technical consistency;
- identifying errors, omissions or unclear statements;
- confirming required corrections are completed before issue;
- recording review where required.

### 6.4 Workers and Contractors

Workers and contractors are responsible for:

- working within their competence;
- following Ax4 procedures and instructions;
- collecting accurate field information;
- raising uncertainty or quality concerns;
- using current templates and controlled documents;
- reporting errors, hazards or nonconformances promptly.

Records must be stored in a manner that supports traceability, confidentiality, retrieval and legal defensibility.
